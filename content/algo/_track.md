@name Algorithms
@brief
- Most loops still have **one or two live coding rounds** of 45 to 60 minutes. Usually that is one medium problem plus a follow-up, or two easier problems.
- They grade four things: **finding the approach**, **clean and correct code**, **testing your own code** and **explaining as you go**. A correct answer in silence often scores lower than a slower one that is explained well.
- Most problems are variations of about **20 patterns**. Each section here starts with a pattern card. Learn that first, then the problems in that section.
- Where AI help is allowed, the problems get bigger and they grade how well you check the AI's code. You still need these patterns to spot a wrong answer quickly.
@tips
- **Clarify first:** input size, empty input, duplicates, negative numbers, what to return.
- **Say the brute force and its Big O**, then improve it. Never jump straight to the code.
- **Name the pattern out loud:** "this is a sliding window problem".
- **Test with the example**, then with edge cases, before you say you are done.
- **Use this track like a real round:** try each problem for 10 to 20 minutes before you open it. All solutions are Python 3 and tested.
